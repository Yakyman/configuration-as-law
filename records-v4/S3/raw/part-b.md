## Fixed facts and what is not a fact

Let D be the last successfully applied declaration, including line L naming outside source R for accounts. In the **source-only reading** of S8.2-18, L supplies this source and no sampling date, expiry period, offline branch, or other temporal rule. This is a candidate reading of the published permission, not a claim that a particular declaration language accepts an incomplete line.

The one illustrative history fixes all values needed to expose the question. At successful application, R's list and the computer's accounts contributed by L are both {account-A}. At the examined offline moment, R's list is {account-B}, the last successfully received list is still {account-A}, and the computer still has account-A, not account-B. No further application has been reported successful; there has been no breaking. Both source values are stipulated known to the argument, even though the offline computer cannot read the later one. All unrelated state is held fixed. The change in R is background history distinguishing two meanings of the same offline requirement, not an additional refresh, removal, or experiment.

Two proposed required states at that **same** offline moment are therefore:

- application-snapshot requirement: accounts contributed by L must be {account-A};
- current-source requirement: accounts contributed by L must be {account-B}.

Neither is adopted. Under the first, the stipulated local list meets the proposed account requirement; under the second, it does not. The source history is identical in both readings. Thus this comparison does not infer a textual gap from an unknown remote fact. The person-visible difference is which account exists on the computer and can be selected for local account administration; it is not a claim that the existing account can authenticate. Credentials, access rules, and permitted login methods are separate settings. S8.1-11: “A service is in force if it is enabled; an account, if it exists.” S8.2-13: “Settings are services and their parameters, the network, including saved networks, devices and filesystems, time and language, accounts, rights, access rules and what is permitted as a means of login to this computer.”

## Attempts to refute the candidate gap

1. **S8.1-12 already says the line states its required state.** This is the strongest objection. Read as a validity requirement, it demands that L have sufficient semantics; an incomplete source-only L need not qualify. P2 then evaluates the requirement given by L, rather than adding a universally fixed freshness policy. On this reading the example has not established a valid underspecified line. The counter-reading is that S8.2-18 expressly permits taking a list by naming its source without saying that the line must additionally specify a time; S8.1-12 does not itself select which time the source means. The fixed text does not expressly settle the relation between these readings. Selecting one here would decide a question reserved for the author by (not published: orchestration or scratch) and `SUP1/step-3.md`.

2. **The declaration language or the implementation may define L exactly.** Agreed: a line need not spell out all its semantics in human prose. If an actual language's documented source primitive means last completed application, most recent successful receipt, current source, or a stated offline algorithm, S8.1-12 and P2 refer to that semantics. Different systems choosing different documented rules are not by themselves a gap. An actual fixture with an unknown language rule is **FACT_UNKNOWN**, with the missing fact named as the documented meaning of L. There is no such real fixture here. The candidate textual problem is whether the hypothesis also permits L with genuinely no temporal rule. It cannot be turned into 0 merely by omitting implementation documentation from an example.

3. **Section 8.4 intentionally relaxes the external-source case.** The relevant consequence, quote: “For a line that takes a list from outside the declaration, the declaration shows which source determines the list.” It requires identification of the source, not full reconstruction of its contents from the declaration alone. The other exception, quote: “For a line that takes a list from outside the declaration, reproducibility is not claimed.” Therefore different remote lists or different machines' outputs are not a reproducibility counterexample here. Neither sentence expressly waives P2 at an offline moment or names a temporal rule. They support delegation to the source and defeat an overbroad determinism objection; they do not by themselves decide whether temporal policy is missing or delegated.

4. **Section 8.5 excludes implementation choices and permits algorithms.** Its relevant passage, quote: “User authority, generations and rollback, protection of the declaration itself and secrets, when an edit takes effect. The form of the declaration: it may be a list or an algorithm.” No declaration edit occurs here, so uncertainty about edit timing is not the issue. The excluded mechanism choices do leave room for a line whose defined algorithm chooses its offline list. They also warn against calling the absence of one universal caching policy a gap. But they do not expressly exempt an admitted line from having a required state. The question remains validity and semantics of the supposed source-only line, not a demand that all implementations share a policy.

5. **A cache is a program record, so its contents are outside the definition.** The argument concerns the account that exists, not a file storing cached bytes. Accounts are expressly settings (S8.2-13). S8.2-15 is reached only after the earlier ordered questions; naming a file a cache cannot by itself remove an account setting from P1/P2/P4. Conversely no cached file is assumed to create an account, and no behavior of a real account service is asserted.

6. **Current remote contents are unknown while offline.** In a real observation that has not collected R's state, this would be **FACT_UNKNOWN**, not a gap. Here both values and times are fixed. The missing candidate rule is which known time P2 addresses. The computer's inability to learn a value is distinct from the reviewer's inability to know which value a requirement selects.

7. **Outside changes are automatically a P4 violation.** No: S8.2-18 expressly allows a named external source. Following a fully specified source rule can be change through the declaration. Conversely P4 supplies no rule selecting snapshot or current contents. The text's delegation cannot simply be ignored to manufacture a contradiction between P2 and P4.

8. **A valid rule can allow more than one list.** Agreed: S8.1-12 and P2 need not demand a unique list across all histories, systems, or permitted outcomes. For example, if a specified line permits either the last received list or the current source list while offline, both candidate lists meet it. That is a defined permission, not an undetermined requirement. The candidate problem is narrower: from the supposed source-only line, can one decide whether the actual retained {account-A} is permitted at all? A rule requiring current contents rejects it, a snapshot rule accepts it, and a rule permitting both accepts it. The argument does not infer a gap from multiplicity. It records that the acceptance question itself has no answer under the stipulated absence of any temporal semantics, while the validity of that stipulated absence is the author-reading question. Nothing here imposes uniqueness as a new property.

## Incompatibility arguments tested and rejected

The tempting chain is: (i) S8.2-18 names R; (ii) infer that its current contents must hold immediately; (iii) S8.3-1 applies offline; (iv) S8.3-5 requires current contents offline; (v) conclude all systems fail because a computer without communication cannot know a remote change. Step (ii) is unsupported unless the disputed current-source reading or a concrete line's semantics is supplied. Step (v) also needs precise communication and prior-knowledge premises. The present history does not prove universal inability; a system could have an explicit snapshot rule. No impossibility result follows from the definitions alone.

Making a whole change indivisible does not answer which list L requires. An atomic successful application can leave a completely defined snapshot, and that defeats any assertion that every conforming system fails this offline event. If L instead requires current remote contents, an atomic *local* update does not alone provide unknown remote information; that is a conditional limitation of the particular rule and information model, not a definition-wide contradiction. The fixed text does not prohibit indivisible application. No multi-step update is introduced into part (b).
